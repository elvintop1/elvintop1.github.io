Chapter 00

How to learn this subject

Quantum computing becomes manageable when every concept is expressed in four connected languages: plain words, linear algebra, a physical experiment, and code. If one representation feels mysterious, move to another and then return.

01

Words

State exactly what is prepared, transformed, and measured.

02

Mathematics

03

Physics

04

Code

The central habit

Never read a circuit only as a picture. Track the state after each gate, identify the basis used for measurement, and check that all probabilities sum to one.

Chapter 01 · Mathematical foundation

Complex numbers and amplitudes

A complex number has a real part and an imaginary part:

$$z=a+ib,\qquad i^2=-1.$$

Its complex conjugate reverses the sign of the imaginary part, $z^*=a-ib$. Multiplying a number by its conjugate gives a non-negative real number:

$$|z|^2=z^*z=a^2+b^2.$$

This is exactly what quantum theory needs. Amplitudes may carry phase, allowing interference, while squared magnitudes become ordinary probabilities.

Polar form and phase

Euler's identity connects circular geometry with complex algebra:

$$e^{i\phi}=\cos\phi+i\sin\phi,\qquad z=re^{i\phi}.$$

The magnitude $r$ controls probability weight. The angle $\phi$ is phase. Multiplying an entire state by $e^{i\gamma}$ is a global phase and changes no measurement probability. Changing only one component changes a relative phase and can alter later interference.

Worked calculation 01

Normalize two complex amplitudes

Take the unnormalized vector $v=(1,1+i)^T$. Its squared norm is

$$\|v\|^2=|1|^2+|1+i|^2=1+2=3.$$

Therefore $|\psi\rangle=v/\sqrt3$. Measurement in this coordinate basis gives

$$p(0)=\frac13,\qquad p(1)=\frac{|1+i|^2}{3}=\frac23.$$

The checkpoint is $p(0)+p(1)=1$.

Check yourself: global or relative phase?

$|\psi\rangle$ and $i|\psi\rangle$ differ only by a global phase. In contrast, $(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ and $(|0\rangle+i|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ differ by a relative phase and can be distinguished by measurements outside the Z basis.

Chapter 02 · Mathematical foundation

Vectors, inner products, and matrices

A ket is a column vector. For a two-dimensional system, choose an orthonormal basis:

$$|0\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}1\\0\end{pmatrix},\qquad |1\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}0\\1\end{pmatrix}.$$

A general vector is a linear combination $|\psi\rangle=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle$. Its bra is the conjugate transpose $\langle\psi|=(\alpha^*,\beta^*)$. The inner product $\langle\phi|\psi\rangle$ is a complex overlap; the outer product $|\psi\rangle\langle\phi|$ is a matrix.

Normalized

$$\langle\psi|\psi\rangle=1$$

The vector has unit length.

Orthogonal

$$\langle\phi|\psi\rangle=0$$

The states can be perfectly distinguished in a suitable measurement.

Hermitian

$$A^\dagger=A$$

An observable has real eigenvalues.

Unitary

$$U^\dagger U=I$$

An isolated evolution preserves inner products and probability.

Eigenvectors and measurement values

If $A|a\rangle=a|a\rangle$, then $|a\rangle$ is an eigenvector and $a$ is its eigenvalue. For a Hermitian observable, the possible projective-measurement values are its eigenvalues. The spectral decomposition makes this explicit:

$$A=\sum_a a|a\rangle\langle a|.$$

The expectation value is the probability-weighted average over many repetitions:

$$\langle A\rangle_\psi=\langle\psi|A|\psi\rangle.$$

Tensor products combine systems

Two two-dimensional systems form a four-dimensional joint system:

$$\mathbb C^2\otimes\mathbb C^2\cong\mathbb C^4,\qquad |0\rangle\otimes|1\rangle=|01\rangle.$$

Always declare the basis order before writing a matrix. This course uses $|00\rangle,|01\rangle,|10\rangle,|11\rangle$ for hand calculations and calls out Qiskit's displayed bit-string convention when it matters.

Worked calculation 02

Prove that Hadamard preserves probability

$$H=\frac1{\sqrt2}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\1&-1\end{pmatrix},\qquad H^\dagger H=I.$$

Because $H$ is real and symmetric, $H^\dagger=H$. Direct multiplication gives $H^2=I$. Therefore $\|H|\psi\rangle\|^2=\langle\psi|H^\dagger H|\psi\rangle=1$ for every normalized input.

Chapter 03 · Probability foundation

Probability, sampling, and uncertainty

A probability distribution assigns non-negative weights summing to one. An expectation is a long-run average, while a variance measures spread:

$$\mathbb E[X]=\sum_x x\,p(x),\qquad \operatorname{Var}(X)=\mathbb E[X^2]-\mathbb E[X]^2.$$

Quantum amplitudes are not probabilities. First project the state onto an outcome and then square the magnitude. If the state is $|\psi\rangle$ and the outcome vector is $|m\rangle$, the Born rule is

$$p(m)=|\langle m|\psi\rangle|^2.$$

On a simulator or device, a shot is one preparation–circuit–measurement trial. If $k$ successes occur in $N$ independent shots, $\hat p=k/N$ estimates the underlying probability. Its typical standard error is approximately

$$\operatorname{SE}(\hat p)\approx\sqrt{\frac{p(1-p)}{N}}.$$
Do not confuse three layers

Exact probability is a model prediction. Finite-shot fluctuation is sampling uncertainty. Hardware noise is a physical deviation from the ideal operation. More shots reduce the second, not automatically the third.

Worked calculation 03

How many shots are enough?

For a balanced outcome with $p=1/2$ and $N=1000$,

$$\operatorname{SE}(\hat p)\approx\sqrt{\frac{0.25}{1000}}\approx0.0158.$$

A frequency near 0.48 or 0.52 is therefore ordinary sampling variation, not evidence that the Born rule failed.

Chapter 04 · Physics foundation

The operational postulates of quantum mechanics

Quantum mechanics is a framework for predicting statistics of physical experiments. For computing, its core can be organized into four rules. They are not derived from classical mechanics; they are the model we test against nature.

01

State

An isolated physical system is represented by a unit vector $|\psi\rangle$ in a complex inner-product space. A density operator $\rho$ handles mixtures and subsystems.

02

Evolution

A closed system evolves unitarily: $|\psi'\rangle=U|\psi\rangle$. In continuous time, $U(t)=e^{-iHt/\hbar}$ for Hamiltonian $H$.

03

Measurement

Measurement operators $\{M_m\}$ satisfy $\sum_mM_m^\dagger M_m=I$. Outcome $m$ has probability $\langle\psi|M_m^\dagger M_m|\psi\rangle$.

04

Composition

The state space of systems A and B is $\mathcal H_A\otimes\mathcal H_B$. This rule permits joint states that cannot be separated into independent subsystem states.

A physical bridge: Stern–Gerlach

Imagine preparing many identically oriented spin-½ particles and sending them through an apparatus that separates two outcomes along a chosen axis. A Z-oriented preparation gives one deterministic Z outcome, while the same preparation measured along X divides into two statistical outcomes. Repeating the procedure reveals that “state” is not a hidden list of predetermined values for every axis; the chosen measurement basis matters.

A qubit abstracts this two-outcome quantum system. $|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$ label a chosen computational basis, not necessarily literal classical voltages. In different hardware they may represent different energy levels, spin states, photon modes, or other controlled degrees of freedom.

Why not say “the qubit is both 0 and 1”?

That phrase hides the most important information: complex amplitudes and the chosen basis. A precise statement is that the state is a normalized linear combination of basis vectors, and different measurements sample distributions calculated from its projections.

Chapter 05 · First quantum model

Derive one qubit from the rules

Choose a two-dimensional basis and write the most general pure state:

$$|\psi\rangle=\alpha|0\rangle+\beta|1\rangle,\qquad |\alpha|^2+|\beta|^2=1.$$

After normalization and removal of one global phase, two real parameters remain. They form the Bloch-sphere representation:

$$|\psi\rangle=\cos\frac\theta2|0\rangle+e^{i\phi}\sin\frac\theta2|1\rangle.$$

A Z-basis measurement returns 0 with probability $\cos^2(\theta/2)$ and 1 with probability $\sin^2(\theta/2)$. The phase $\phi$ is invisible in Z populations but appears in X or Y statistics.

Worked calculation 04

Phase becomes population through interference

The states $|+\rangle=(|0\rangle+|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ and $|-\rangle=(|0\rangle-|1\rangle)/\sqrt2$ both look balanced in Z. Apply $H$:

$$H|+\rangle=|0\rangle,\qquad H|-\rangle=|1\rangle.$$

The final measurement is deterministic and different. The sign was not a hidden probability; it was relative phase that controlled interference.

From one qubit to entanglement

Start with $|00\rangle$. Apply H to the first qubit and CNOT from the first to the second:

$$|00\rangle\xrightarrow{H\otimes I}\frac{|00\rangle+|10\rangle}{\sqrt2}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{CNOT}}\frac{|00\rangle+|11\rangle}{\sqrt2}.$$

The final state cannot be written as $|a\rangle\otimes|b\rangle$. Each qubit alone is maximally mixed, but the pair has perfect joint correlations. This distinction between local and joint information powers teleportation, superdense coding, and error correction.

Lab A · statevector before shots

Python · Qiskit SDK
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.quantum_info import Statevector

qc = QuantumCircuit(1)
qc.h(0)

state = Statevector.from_instruction(qc)
print(state)
print(state.probabilities_dict())

state.seed(42)
print(state.sample_counts(1000))
  1. Predict the two amplitudes before running the code.
  2. Why are the exact probabilities equal while the two counts need not be identical?
  3. Replace H with Ry(π/3). Calculate the answer before changing the code.
Lab A · expected reasoning

$H|0\rangle=|+\rangle$, so both amplitudes are $1/\sqrt2$ and both exact probabilities are 1/2. Counts fluctuate because 1,000 shots are a finite random sample. For $R_y(\pi/3)|0\rangle$, probabilities are $\cos^2(\pi/6)=3/4$ and $\sin^2(\pi/6)=1/4$.

Lab B · Bell state with the V2 sampler

Python · Qiskit SDK
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.primitives import StatevectorSampler

bell = QuantumCircuit(2)
bell.h(0)
bell.cx(0, 1)
bell.measure_all()

sampler = StatevectorSampler(seed=42)
result = sampler.run([bell], shots=1000).result()
counts = result[0].data.meas.get_counts()
print(counts)

The ideal results contain 00 and 11 but not 01 or 10. Do not summarize this as “both bits were known in advance.” The joint state predicts correlations, while each local outcome remains random.

Lab C · exact expectation value

Python · quantum_info
from qiskit import QuantumCircuit
from qiskit.quantum_info import Pauli, Statevector

qc = QuantumCircuit(1)
qc.ry(0.8, 0)
qc.rz(0.4, 0)
state = Statevector.from_instruction(qc)

for label in ["X", "Y", "Z"]:
    value = state.expectation_value(Pauli(label)).real
    print(label, value)

These three expectations are the Bloch-vector coordinates. Check that $\langle X\rangle^2+\langle Y\rangle^2+\langle Z\rangle^2=1$ for this pure state.
